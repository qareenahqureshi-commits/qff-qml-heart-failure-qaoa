The idea, plainly

From a hospital spreadsheet to a quantum circuit and back

1 · data

299 patients

Heart-failure records, Faisalabad 2015. 96 died in follow-up. 11 routine measurements each.

2 · question

Pick a panel

Choose 3 of 11 measurements: 165 options. Which predicts survival best?

3 · score

Useful, not repetitive

Points for separating deaths from survivors, minus points for overlap. That score is a QUBO, the input quantum optimisers read.

4 · quantum

Ask QAOA

One qubit per measurement. The circuit makes good panels likelier to appear when measured. 2000 shots.

5 · check

Verify

Brute force, classical solvers, a published study, a noisy-chip model, and leakage-free cross-validation.

Under the hood · the QAOA circuit

What the quantum computer actually runs

Start every qubit in an equal mix of selected and not selected (H gates). Then repeat two steps p times: a cost layer that rewards useful measurements and penalises overlapping pairs (one ZZ gate per pair, 55 pairs), and a mixer layer that lets qubits change their mind. Classical COBYLA tunes the angles γ and β. Finally, measure.

Straight talk

What this shows and what it does not

Shows

  • Age, ejection fraction and serum creatinine beat all 11 measurements (MCC 0.430 vs 0.365, paired p = 1e-8).
  • QAOA's best sample is the true optimum in 59 of 60 runs at depth 2, and stays in the top 5 under realistic chip noise.
  • Greedy selection on the same score fails (MCC 0.225); judging panels jointly fixes it.

Does not show

  • Quantum advantage: classical simulated annealing solves this 11-variable problem perfectly in 0.3 s.
  • A clinical tool: 299 patients, one hospital; choosing panel size without peeking gives MCC 0.391.
  • Real-hardware runs yet: the noise study uses IBM's calibration model of a real chip.
